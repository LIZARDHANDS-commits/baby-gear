Is the market overheated?

Read more

We use the four warning tests Canada's housing agency (CMHC) uses, adapted to the Valley. Green means no warning, yellow means be careful, red means don't overpay. Today: no test is yellow or red, but only 2 of the 4 have full data.

How the four tests work, and the numbers Show more
  • Overheating: are homes selling faster than new ones come up? Sales ÷ new listings above about 70% favours sellers. We have no new-listing counts yet, so we use months of supply and days on market as stand-ins.
  • Price acceleration: is the yearly price rise speeding up quarter after quarter? Yellow if it speeds up 3 quarters running; red if 4 or more and the rise tops 10% a year.
  • Overvaluation: does owning cost far more than renting a similar home, or are prices far above local incomes? Only the sale-price-to-assessment part has data so far.
  • Overbuilding: are unsold new builds or empty rentals piling up? That would mean prices could soften.

The lines are adapted from CMHC's Housing Market Assessment. No official test exists for a market as small as the Valley, so we read them over 6 to 12 months and treat them as signals, not proof.

The research, most useful first.

Market and price bands Show more

In short: The big jump came in 2020 to 2022. Since then prices have kept rising at a steady pace of about 5% a year, with fewer sales, not a frenzy. Pricier homes have gone up less than cheaper ones.

  • The Valley median sale price (detached homes, Middleton to Kentville) was $325,000 in 2022 and $399,200 in 2026 so far (to 28 Oct), up 22.8%. It rose every year after 2022; there was no drop to wait for.1
  • No sign of a speculative run-up. Sales are about 26% below 2022 and 5% below 2025 for the same dates. Fewer sales with slowly rising prices looks like a tight, slow market, not quick flipping.1
  • Pat's question: yes, partly. Since 2022 the top fifth of sales rose 13.2%, against 37.5% for the cheapest fifth. The evidence for the gap is moderate, not decisive.1
  • Homes like ours (3+ bed, 2+ bath, garage, 1,400 to 2,600 sq ft) sold for a median of $497,250 in 2025 to 2026 (366 sales). 73% sold between $425,000 and $700,000.1
  • The monthly check shows no red or yellow flags, but only 2 of its 4 tests have data. Not an all-clear yet.3

Costs, taxes and buyer programs Show more

In short: Deed transfer tax is the one big closing tax, at 1.5% in most of the Valley. The FHSA can only be opened from 1 Jan 2027, and its tax refund comes after a spring 2027 purchase.

  • Deed transfer tax is 1.5% in Kings County, Annapolis County, Middleton, Kentville and Wolfville. It is 1.25% in Berwick.1
  • Yearly property tax for 2026-27 is $0.853 per $100 of assessed value in Kings County and $1.46620 per $100 in Kentville.2
  • The FHSA gives $8,000 of room in the first year. Both of us can open one on or after 1 Jan 2027. This is our reading of the rules, not yet confirmed by the tax agency.4
  • The Home Buyers' Plan lets each person take out up to $60,000 from RRSPs, and it can be combined with the FHSA for one purchase.5
  • The move benefits cover some closing costs and inspections, if we close inside the allowed window.6

Lawyer and inspection fees also apply at closing. Our sources give only rough, secondary ranges, so we leave out the numbers.6

Yearly property tax

The bill is the rate times the assessed value, divided by 100. Nova Scotia caps assessment rises (2.6% for 2026), but the cap is removed the year after a sale, so a new owner's assessment can jump to market value before the cap restarts.7

FHSA basics and timing

  • To open one, you must not have lived in a home you owned (or your spouse owned) in this year or the previous 4 calendar years.4
  • The same look-back is repeated when you withdraw, for your own home only. Moving back into a home we own before withdrawing would fail the test.3
  • The home must not have been acquired more than 30 days before the withdrawal.3
  • A rental you have never lived in may not block opening. No case confirms this, so we will ask the tax agency in writing.8
  • Only the account holder deducts the contribution. The refund comes with the 2027 tax return, in spring 2028.4

Home Buyers' Plan and other programs

  • The home must become your main home within one year. Repayment timing is not yet confirmed.5
  • The Home Buyers' Amount is a $10,000 credit, worth about $1,500. The official page is not yet read.5
  • New builds only: a federal GST rebate of up to $50,000 and a Nova Scotia rebate of up to $3,000.5

What the move benefits cover

  • Window: closing no more than 1 year before, or 2 years after, the move date (or the household goods load date, if later).6
  • Renting first and buying later keeps the purchase benefits, less any advance-rent and rental-finder reimbursements. The rental-to-house move is not paid.6
  • Reimbursed: legal fees including deed transfer tax, survey and title insurance, and the main inspections (structural, heating, well and septic).6

Renting, lenders and agents Show more

In short: Base housing is the cheapest way to rent, at $747 to $921 a month for a 3-bedroom, but its wait time is not published. A lender will test any mortgage at a higher "stress" rate, so ask for pre-approval early.

  • Base housing 3-bedroom charges average $747 (row), $914 (semi) and $921 (single). Utilities are extra.1
  • Private rent: a Halifax 2-bedroom turnover rent was $2,058 in Oct 2025, down from $2,116.2
  • Stress test: lenders qualify you at your contract rate plus 2%, or 5.25%, whichever is greater.3
  • A pre-approved rate is guaranteed for up to 120 days at one big bank, but it is not a commitment to lend.4
  • Keeping a rental while buying elsewhere has trade-offs on lending, tax and tenant rules.35

Pre-approval and the stress test

A pre-approval is a lender's early check of what you could borrow. The stress test asks whether you could still pay if rates were higher. For a mortgage with a big down payment (uninsured), the test rate is the contract rate plus 2% or 5.25%, whichever is greater.3

For an insured mortgage (small down payment) the limits are:3

  • Credit score of at least 600.
  • 25-year amortization (the years to pay off the loan) and a price below $1,500,000.
  • The buyer must live in the home.
  • Minimum down payment is 5% of the first $500,000 plus 10% of the rest.

How a lender counts rent from another property is not yet confirmed. Our best reading of the insurer's rules is that only rent minus its costs would count, and that this approach is not often used. We will ask both lenders.3 Usual papers are a pay stub, an employment letter and a direct-deposit statement.4

A buyer's agent

A buyer's agent finds listings, books viewings and helps with offers. Free searching helps too: sold prices need a free sign-up on the listing site, and the province's sales data can be read without a key.6 Our sources do not give an agent's cost, so we have not confirmed it. Some relocation programs pay participating agents, and costs above the government rate may not be refunded.6

Keep or sell a rental home: the general trade-offs

  • Rent is income, and interest, tax, insurance and management fees can be deducted.5
  • Only one property per family can be named the principal residence for a given year. That affects tax when you later sell.5
  • Nova Scotia caps rent rises at 5% until 31 Dec 2027.5

Checking a house and its price Show more

In short: Nova Scotia has no septic inspection at sale, so we test radon, water and the oil tank ourselves and write them into the offer as conditions. For price, we compare recorded sales, but the public data is thin, so the price method is still being built.

  • Radon: about 36.8% of Nova Scotia homes are expected to be above 200 Bq/m3, against 17.8% across Canada. There is no figure for Kings or Annapolis county. Test for at least 91 days.1
  • Septic: there is no inspection when a house is sold. An owner must report a malfunction within 15 days, and only certified installers may do the work.1
  • Wells: test for bacteria every year and for chemistry every five years.1
  • In the sales data, 25,727 of 67,394 sales since 1 January 2025 show a price of 0, so some sales cannot be used.2

Checks before an offer

  • Radon: the guideline is 200 Bq/m3. If a home is over it, fix it within 1 year.1
  • Well water: the minimum test covers arsenic, uranium, manganese, lead, nitrate, fluoride and hardness. Health Canada limits include arsenic 0.010, uranium 0.02 and no E. coli at all.1
  • Oil tank: it must be installed by a licensed Oil Burner Technician, and used tanks are not allowed. Corrosion causes 35-45% of failures. The provincial checklist asks if the tank is under 15 years old. An insurer rule of 20-25 years is not yet confirmed.1
  • Noise: Kings County planning limits building next to the airport, especially in the NEF 35 and above zone. NEF is a measure of aircraft noise.1

Forms and conditions

  • The Property Condition Disclosure Statement (Form 211) is optional. A buyer can ask for it as a condition. Form 431 is the water and septic schedule. Since 1 July 2025 it can also ask about flooding, coastal erosion and drainage.1
  • An agreement has dated conditions for inspection, financing and a lawyer. The deposit comes back if a valid financing condition is used in time. This comes from a secondary source. After conditions are removed, the deal is binding and the deposit is at risk.1

Judging a fair price

The plan is to compare a home with recent recorded sales nearby. The sales table has price, date and location only. Size, style, age and bedrooms come from a separate assessment file, which may be out of date. It holds nothing on condition or renovations. Sales at 0 may hide family sales, which are not true market prices.2

An assessment is a useful cross-check, but it reflects market value on 1 January of the prior year. The assessor also warns that a sale price may not match market value.2

Sellers who would sell at a loss tend to ask 25-35% of the gap above the expected price.2

Not yet confirmed: the sources do not set out the exact time adjustment, the walk-away price rule or the number of calibration viewings. Those steps are still to be tested. People tend to stop looking too early, so viewing a first batch of homes without buying is the idea; the "37%" rule of thumb is from research, not tested here.2

Costs for wells, treatment, septic, tanks, radon systems, rewiring and foundations are rough ranges and not yet confirmed.1

Quick facts

What to look for in a Valley house Show more
  • Radon: about 37% of Nova Scotia homes are expected to test above the 200 Bq/m³ guideline. Test for at least 91 days, or ask for results.
  • Oil tanks: the provincial checklist asks whether the tank is under 15 years old. Ask the age and get an insurance quote before an offer.
  • Wells and septic: test well water for bacteria every year and chemistry every 5 years. Nova Scotia has no septic inspection at sale, so ask for the records.
  • Deed transfer tax: 1.5% of the price in most of the Valley (Berwick 1.25%). The move benefits reimburse it if we close inside the window.
  • Property tax: it jumps after a sale because the assessment cap is removed the next year. Budget the uncapped amount.
  • Aircraft noise: parts of Kings County near the airfield limit building; check the noise map for any lot.
First Home Savings Account (FHSA) Show more
  • Each of us can open one on or after 1 January 2027 and put in $8,000, tax-deductible. Opening in December 2026 would fail the look-back test.
  • The money comes out tax-free for the purchase if we take it out no more than 30 days after we acquire the home.
  • The tax refund arrives with the 2027 return (spring 2028), so it doesn't help the down payment itself.
  • Owning a rental we don't live in should not block us, but no official case confirms it. We'll ask CRA in writing before relying on it.
How we judge a fair price Show more
  • Find 3 or more recorded sales of similar homes nearby (size, beds, baths, garage, age). Under 3 means no basis for an offer.
  • Adjust for time. Prices are rising about 5% a year right now; the exact adjustment is still being worked out.
  • Cross-check against the assessed value. In 2026, sales run about 16% above assessment on average.
  • Write a walk-away price before any viewing, and never go past it. This is our rule; it hasn't been tested on real homes yet.
